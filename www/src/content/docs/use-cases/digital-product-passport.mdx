The EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) introduces Digital Product Passports:
verifiable records of a regulated product's materials, carbon footprint, recycled content and
origin. Regulators, recyclers and consumers need to trust those claims. But the exact numbers
behind them are commercially sensitive. A precise carbon figure reveals manufacturing efficiency,
origin data reveals suppliers, and inspection records reveal defect rates. The tension is real:
*prove it, but don't publish it.*

## The zero-knowledge idea

**The auditor seals each measurement in a Pedersen commitment. The manufacturer proves "the value
in this commitment is at most 50 kg CO₂e" (or "at least 30 % recycled", "an EU country", "three
approved inspections in order") without opening it.**

The flow, in plain terms:

- **The auditor commits.** It measures the product and, for each claim, computes a
  `pedersen-jubjub-vector-v1` commitment `C = (u, v)` with a fresh 252-bit random blinding `r`.
  It gives the opening `(m, r)` (value and blinding) to the manufacturer with its report.
- **The manufacturer proves.** For each claim, a Groth16 proof shows that the value inside `C`
  passes the rule. A claim that doesn't hold has no proof at all.
- **One transaction mints the passport.** The manufacturer builds it, the auditor co-signs it, and
  the `DppPassport` script mints a passport token into a locked output whose datum holds every
  commitment the auditor attests.
- **Anyone reads it.** Customs, a buyer or a regulator finds the passport on-chain and checks each
  claim's proof against the commitments there. The carbon proof was checked on-chain at mint; the
  manufacturer publishes the other proofs off-chain (in the demo, its server serves them). Only the
  auditor can revoke it.

If commitments are new to you, [Hide a number](/selective-disclosure/hide-a-number/) explains them
with an interactive envelope.

## Why not just hash the measurement?

The first version of this demo published `auditorHash = Poseidon(auditorSecret, Poseidon(productId,
m))` in the passport datum, where `m` is the measurement. It leaked in two ways:

- **The key was not secret.** `auditorSecret` was an input to the manufacturer's proof, so every
  manufacturer had to hold it, and the demo hard-coded it (`777777`) in its source.
- **The value was easy to guess.** Carbon and recycled content are 16-bit numbers, and a country
  is one of about 250 codes. Anyone could hash all 65,536 candidates and read `m` off the chain.
  A hash of a small value with a known key hides nothing.

A Pedersen commitment fixes both. The blinding is fresh for each commitment and is never shared
between manufacturers, and for every possible value there is some blinding that gives the same
point, so the commitment says nothing about the value. Try the search yourself:

<PassportPrivacyExplainer />

<TextOnly>

| Published in the datum | Can an observer recover the carbon value? |
|---|---|
| `auditorHash = Poseidon(auditorSecret, Poseidon(productId, carbon))`, with `auditorSecret = 777777` in the demo source | Yes: hash all 65,536 values and compare |
| `C = (u, v)`, a `pedersen-jubjub-vector-v1` commitment with a fresh 252-bit blinding | No: every value matches some blinding |

</TextOnly>

The old design had binding gaps too. The minting policy fixed neither the threshold nor
`auditorHash`, so a manufacturer could prove against a threshold, or a value, of its own choosing.
The token name was not tied to the product and could be minted again, and the demo's registry and
`/api/dpp/status` returned every measurement. The demo's `PassportBruteForceTest` runs the
65,536-value search: it recovers the carbon value from an old datum and nothing from a new
passport. The redesign is
[ADR-0009](https://github.com/bloxbean/zeroj-usecases/blob/main/docs/adr/0009-privacy-and-binding-hardening-of-the-original-demos.md)
D1.

## What stays private, what's public

| Value | Visibility | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Measurements (`carbon_kg`, `recycled_pct`, `country`, inspector ids and times) | Secret | The competitive data; held by the auditor and the manufacturer |
| Blinding `r` (one per claim, 252 bits) | Secret | Fresh from `SecureRandom`; it's what makes the commitment hide the value |
| `productId` | Public | The token name and the first datum field; ties the passport to one product |
| `manufacturerPkh` | Public | In the datum, and fixed by the script |
| Each claim's `(claim, σ, u, v)` | Public | The commitment `(u, v)` and its schema digest `σ`, in the datum |
| Threshold (50 kg) and approved-set roots | Public | Fixed by the script (carbon) or by the reader (the other claims) |

`σ` is the digest of the claim's schema. A commitment point doesn't record what its values mean,
so every proof binds `σ` as a public input, and every verifier takes the expected `σ` from its own
copy of the schemas.

## The four claims

Each claim is one commitment under its own schema. The labels and bit widths are fixed by the
design:

| Claim (id) | Schema | Circuit | Public inputs, in order | Rule | Constraints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon (0) | `zeroj.demo.dpp.carbon` v1: `carbon_kg`/16 | `CarbonClaimProof` | `σ, u, v, threshold` | `carbon_kg ≤ threshold` | 2,192 |
| Recycled content (1) | `zeroj.demo.dpp.recycled` v1: `recycled_pct`/16 | `RecycledClaimProof` | `σ, u, v, threshold` | `recycled_pct ≥ threshold` | 2,192 |
| Origin (2) | `zeroj.demo.dpp.origin` v1: `country`/16 | `OriginClaimProof` | `σ, u, v, countryRoot` | `country` is a leaf of the approved-set tree (depth 5, the 27 EU states) | 3,344 |
| Inspections (3) | `zeroj.demo.dpp.inspections` v1: `inspector_0`/8, `time_0`/32, … `inspector_2`/8, `time_2`/32 | `InspectionClaimProof` | `σ, u, v, inspectorRoot` | each inspector is a leaf of the approved-inspector tree (depth 3), and `time_0 < time_1 < time_2` | 5,094 |

- **No shared secret, no `isCompliant`.** Every rule is an assertion, so a failing measurement
  simply can't be proved.
- **The auditor commits only passed inspections**, so there is no `passed` entry. An inspector may
  appear at more than one checkpoint.
- **Tree padding.** Unused leaves hold `2^k`, where `k` is the entry's width (`2^16` for countries,
  `2^8` for inspectors). A committed value is at most `k` bits, so it can never equal padding. The
  old trees padded with 0, which made 0 a member of every set.

## The circuit

The carbon claim, as in the demo's `CarbonClaimProof`. It binds the schema, opens the commitment
in-circuit (which also range-checks `carbon_kg` to 16 bits) and asserts the rule:

```java title="CarbonClaimProof.java"
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.Prove;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.Public;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.Secret;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.UInt;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.ZKCircuit;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.ZkContext;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.ZkField;
import org.zeroj.circuit.annotation.ZkUInt;
import org.zeroj.circuit.lib.jubjub.PedersenVectorSchema;
import org.zeroj.circuit.lib.jubjub.PedersenVectorSchema.Entry;
import org.zeroj.circuit.lib.zk.ZkPedersenVector;

import java.util.List;

@ZKCircuit(name = "dpp-carbon-claim", version = 1)
public class CarbonClaimProof {

    public static final PedersenVectorSchema SCHEMA = PedersenVectorSchema.of("zeroj.demo.dpp.carbon", 1,
            List.of(new Entry("carbon_kg", 16)));

    @Prove
    void prove(ZkContext zk,
               @Public ZkField schemaDigest,                     // σ
               @Public ZkField u,                                // the commitment C = (u, v)
               @Public ZkField v,
               @Public @UInt(bits = 16) ZkUInt threshold,
               @Secret @UInt(bits = 16) ZkUInt carbonKg,          // never revealed
               @Secret @UInt(bits = 252) ZkUInt blinding) {       // the auditor's fresh blinding
        var binding = ZkPedersenVector.bindSchema(zk, SCHEMA, schemaDigest);
        ZkPedersenVector.commit(zk, binding, List.of(carbonKg), blinding).assertAffineEquals(zk, u, v);
        carbonKg.lte(threshold).assertTrue();
    }
}
```

The auditor's side, lightly simplified from the demo's `ClaimOpening`: one commitment per claim,
each with its own blinding.

```java title="Auditor: commit to a measurement"
import org.zeroj.circuit.lib.jubjub.JubjubPoint;
import org.zeroj.circuit.lib.jubjub.PedersenCommitment;
import org.zeroj.circuit.lib.jubjub.PedersenVectorCommitment;

BigInteger carbonKg = BigInteger.valueOf(7);
BigInteger r = PedersenCommitment.randomBlinding(new SecureRandom());   // fresh, 252 bits
JubjubPoint c = PedersenVectorCommitment.commit(CarbonClaimProof.SCHEMA, List.of(carbonKg), r).point();
// (c.affineU(), c.affineV()) goes into the passport datum; (carbonKg, r) goes to the manufacturer
```

The other three circuits follow the same pattern with `ZkMerkle` membership and ordering checks.
See [Commit to several values at once](/guides/confidential/pedersen/#5-commit-to-several-values-at-once)
for vector commitments in general.

## On Cardano

`DppPassport` is a JuLC multi-validator built on `Groth16BLS12381Lib.verify(...)`. Its hash is
both the passport **minting policy** and the passport **address**, so a passport is a public record
that stays where it was minted, not a tradeable token. Its parameters are `manufacturerPkh`,
`auditorPkh`, the gated claim (carbon) with its `σ` and threshold (50 kg), and the carbon circuit's
verification key.

It mints one token named `productId` into an output at its own address with the inline datum

```text
Passport(productId: B, manufacturerPkh: B28, claims: List<Claim(claim: I, σ: B32, u: I, v: I)>)
```

listing every claim the auditor attests (at most 4). The mint redeemer carries only the proof.

**Mint checks:**

- **(a)** the manufacturer and the auditor both sign;
- **(b)** the mint under this policy is exactly `{productId: 1}`, with a 1–32 byte `productId`;
- **(c)** exactly one output holds the token: at the passport address with no stake credential,
  no reference script, only ADA and the token, and an inline datum of exactly the shape above. The
  product id equals the token name, `manufacturerPkh` equals the parameter, every claim has
  canonical `u, v` and a 32-byte `σ`, claim ids are distinct, and the carbon claim is present with
  the parameter `σ`;
- **(d)** the Groth16 proof verifies against `[σ, u, v, threshold]`, with `u, v` from the carbon
  entry and `σ`, threshold from the parameters, never from the manufacturer.

**Revoke.** The only way to spend a passport: the auditor signs and the transaction burns exactly
that passport's token, `{productId: −1}`. A passport is never moved and its datum never changes, so
the datum a reader sees is the one the mint checked.

**The auditor's co-signature.** Before signing, the auditor's `CoSignGuard` checks the finished
transaction: the mint and the passport output are exactly as above, the datum is exactly the
auditor's own commitments for this product and manufacturer, the auditor's key authorises nothing
else (no input or collateral of its own, no withdrawal, certificate or vote), the transaction has a
TTL (the demo sets 180 slots), and the policy has never minted this product. It then reserves the
product for that transaction in one atomic step and only then signs, so concurrent or retried
requests get at most one signature. A reservation is released only after its TTL has passed, so a
failed or timed-out mint blocks a retry for that product for up to three minutes.

| Transaction | CPU steps | % CPU | Memory | % memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Issue (mint, Groth16 verification, 4 claims) | 2,789,042,135 | 27.9 % | 1,390,848 | 8.4 % |
| Revoke (spend and burn) | 151,057,197 | 1.5 % | 561,045 | 3.4 % |

Shares of the per-transaction limits, as printed by the demo's `DppPassportVmTest`.

### Reading a passport

Only carbon is checked on-chain. Recycled content, origin and inspections are proved off-chain,
against the commitments in the passport datum. That datum is the authenticated record of who issued
the commitments: it exists only at the passport address, minted in a transaction the auditor
signed. A reader never accepts a commitment from the manufacturer's API. The demo's
`PassportReader`:

- looks for the passport token at the passport address, and nowhere else;
- reads the policy's whole mint history for the token and refuses a product with more than one
  passport ever minted, including a burn followed by a re-mint. If the history is unavailable, it
  refuses;
- takes each claim's expected `σ` from its own schema registry and never from the datum;
- verifies each off-chain claim's proof against `[σ, u, v, threshold or root]`. In the demo the
  reader endpoint takes those proofs from the manufacturer's records on the same server.

A valid proof is not authorization. The passport's trust comes from the script parameters and the
auditor's signature, not from the proof alone; see
[Application security](/guides/verifying/application-security/).

## Who is trusted for what

| Role | Does | Trusted for |
|---|---|---|
| **Auditor** | Measures, commits each claim with a fresh blinding, gives the openings to the manufacturer, co-signs the passport transaction after checking it against its report, can revoke | Measuring honestly, keeping measurements confidential, co-signing at most one passport per product |
| **Manufacturer** | Proves claims from the openings and builds the passport transaction | Nothing about its measurements |
| **Reader** (customs, buyer, regulator) | Reads the passport from the chain and verifies claim proofs against its commitments | — |

## Limitations

- **The auditor sees every measurement**, could collude with a manufacturer, and alone ensures one
  passport per product, by co-signing once. A second passport under the same policy would be
  publicly visible, and readers refuse that product.
- **The reader's duplicate check needs Yaci Store.** The mint history is read in Yaci Store's
  format, so the reader's check and the auditor's work on DevKit only. Against Blockfrost they
  refuse rather than guess.
- **A passport shows which product has which claims**, but not the values. A very small approved
  set (one country) would reveal the value.
- **Custodial demo.** The demo server holds the manufacturer's and the auditor's keys and the
  openings, and its API is the operator's view. Privacy holds against chain and mempool observers,
  not against this server. The API returns no measurements outside the panel labelled
  "manufacturer (private)".
- **Giving openings to a regulator** is not part of the demo yet; vector openings need their own
  reviewed design.
- **Development setup.** The circuit keys come from a single-party setup; production needs an MPC
  ceremony ([Trusted setup, explained](/learn/trusted-setup/)).
- **Not externally reviewed.** Vector commitments are experimental in ZeroJ, and the demo hasn't
  been audited.

## Security considerations

- **Bind every claim to an attested value.** A proof that "some country is in the approved set"
  proves nothing about *this* product. Here each proof opens a commitment that sits in the
  product's own passport, which only the auditor could co-sign.
- **Thresholds and roots belong to the verifier.** The carbon threshold is a script parameter, so
  a proof for a looser threshold is refused. Off-chain readers use their own thresholds and roots.
- **Never reuse a blinding.** The same value with the same blinding gives the same point, which
  links products. The auditor draws a new one for every claim of every product.
- **Linkability of batches.** Per-batch passports reveal batch sizes and timing. Decide what you're
  willing to publish.

## Try it

The demo lives in
[`digital-product-passport`](https://github.com/bloxbean/zeroj-usecases/tree/main/digital-product-passport).
With Yaci DevKit running (see [Run the demos](/use-cases/overview/#run-the-demos)):

```bash
./demo.sh dpp --run
```

`--run` has the auditor attest battery `BAT-SN001`, the manufacturer prove its claims, mints its
passport and prints the status. In the UI (port 8088):

- **`BAT-SN003`** (65 kg, 20 %) has no carbon proof, so no passport. "Skip off-chain check" builds
  the transaction with a proof for a threshold the manufacturer chose (65,535). The demo runs the
  script locally before the auditor co-signs; the script refuses it, and nothing is submitted.
- **Textile batch `TEX-B2024-003`** (made in China, 5 % recycled) gets a passport, because its
  carbon passes, but its recycled-content and origin claims can't be proved, and the reader shows
  no proof for them.
- **Revoke (auditor)** burns a passport. The demo's DevKit end-to-end test also shows that a
  transfer, or a revoke by the manufacturer, is refused.

The measurements appear only in the "manufacturer (private)" panel. Demo state from the old
version isn't read: start fresh with `./demo.sh dpp --clean-cache`.
